Presenting my paper on balancing faith commitment and academic objectivity in comparative-religion teaching

At the 3rd Islamic and Malay Studies International Conference (IMSIC 2026), I presented my English-language paper, “Teaching Comparative Religion in Islamic Higher Education: Balancing Faith Commitment and Academic Objectivity.” The study asks how Muslim instructors can retain theological commitment while representing other religions accurately, fairly, and according to defensible academic standards.

HostSTAIN Sultan Abdurrahman Kepulauan Riau
Date11–12 November 2026
Conference trackIslamic Education and Learning
Paper languageEnglish
Paper title

Teaching Comparative Religion in Islamic Higher Education: Balancing Faith Commitment and Academic Objectivity

The paper distinguishes three academic activities: accurate description of religious beliefs, evidence-based comparison, and explicitly identified theological evaluation. Its central argument is that academic fairness does not require treating all religions as equally true, while confessional commitment does not justify misrepresenting them.

The proposed framework

Transparency of standpoint

The instructor clearly states the course’s institutional and theological standpoint.

Faithful representation

Each tradition is presented through reliable sources and in terms informed adherents would recognise.

Separate description and judgment

Descriptive accounts are kept distinct from confessional and theological evaluation.

Academic assessment

Students are assessed for accuracy, evidence, and reasoned argument rather than mere agreement.

قدمت في المؤتمر الدولي الثالث للدراسات الإسلامية والملايوية (IMSIC 2026) بحثي باللغة الإنجليزية بعنوان “Teaching Comparative Religion in Islamic Higher Education: Balancing Faith Commitment and Academic Objectivity.” ويتناول البحث سؤالاً تربوياً: كيف يحافظ المدرس المسلم على التزامه العقدي، وفي الوقت نفسه يعرض الأديان الأخرى بدقة وإنصاف ووفق معايير أكاديمية قابلة للدفاع؟
